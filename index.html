Welcome Message

ICIPS 2026

2nd Edition

Following the success of its first edition in 2025, the International Conference on Informatics and Problem Solving (ICIPS 2026) returns for its 2nd edition. Affiliated with the Arab & African Collegiate Programming Contest (ACPC), it is designed to be a dynamic platform that brings together a diverse community of educators, researchers, coaches, and students. Its main purpose is to foster dialogue, collaboration, and the exchange of innovative practices in the fields of informatics education, competitive programming, and problem-solving methodologies. By creating an environment where academic theory meets practical coaching strategies, the conference aims to bridge the gap between classrooms, research labs, and programming arenas.

The 2026 edition focuses on the theme Innovating Informatics Education for the Future, highlighting the urgent need to reimagine how informatics is taught and practiced in a rapidly evolving digital era. Through keynote speeches, research presentations, workshops, and panel discussions, ICIPS provides opportunities to explore modern pedagogical tools, effective competition training techniques, and future directions for integrating computational thinking into various educational contexts. By gathering participants from different regions and backgrounds, the conference also reinforces cultural and academic exchange, making it not only a scientific event but also a hub for inspiring the next generation of problem solvers and innovators.

ICIPS 2026, in collaboration with ACPC, warmly welcomes all participants to an engaging environment where innovation in informatics education meets the spirit of competition. With its focus on the theme "Innovating Informatics Education for the Future", the conference invites educators, researchers, and students to share knowledge, build networks, and inspire new approaches to teaching, problem solving, and coaching in programming contests.

H.E. Yousry S. Elgamal

Professor of Computer Engineering, senior advisor at the Arab Academy for Science and Technology, and Chairman of the Computer Scientific Society (CSS), Alexandria, Egypt. He served as Minister of Education of Egypt (2005–2010), Chairman of the Board of Trustees of the Egypt-Japan University of Science and Technology (E-JUST, 2010–2014), and senior consultant of the National Telecommunications Institute of Egypt. He is a member of the group of experts preparing the Global Knowledge Index and Chairman of the Information and Communication Committee at the National Committee of Education, Culture, and Science (UNESCO, ALECSO, ISESCO), and Editor-in-Chief of the Advances in Computing and Engineering (ACE) journal. He received his B.Sc. in Electrical Engineering from the University of Alexandria (1968), his M.Sc. from Ain Shams University (1977), and a D.Sc. in Computer Science from George Washington University (1985), and is a recipient of the Richard Merwin Award (1984).

Prof. Ashraf M. A. Ahmad

A distinguished academic and innovation leader in Artificial Intelligence, Cybersecurity, and Data Science, with over two decades of experience. He is a Professor of Computer Science at Princess Sumaya University for Technology (PSUT) in Jordan and plays a key role in shaping Jordan's digital transformation through national strategic committees, including the Central Bank of Jordan's Emerging Technologies Committee and initiatives of the Ministry of Digital Economy and Entrepreneurship (MODEE). His research spans explainable AI for finance, lightweight cryptography, and AI-driven content moderation. He is also the founder of the Jordan Collegiate Programming Contest (JCPC) and co-led the first Armython cybersecurity competition in the Middle East & Africa.

Waleed Khalil

Managing Partner at Den VC and a prominent figure in engineering, entrepreneurship, and venture capital, with over two decades of experience across Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Egypt. In early 2023 he founded Den VC, an early-stage venture capital firm investing in deep tech startups across Africa and the Middle East. Previously a partner at Endure Capital (2016–2022), where he helped secure investments leading to four unicorns: Careem, Boom Supersonic, Tribal Credit, and Aspect Biosystems. He holds a bachelor's degree in electronics engineering from Cairo University and an MBA in finance from the American University in Cairo, where he serves as a Faculty Adjunct at the School of Business.

Prof. Faisal N. Abu-Khzam

Professor of Computer Science and Assistant Dean for Research Collaboration and Partnerships at the Lebanese American University (LAU), and director of LAU's online M.S. in Computer Science program and founding director of the Lebanese Collegiate Programming Contest (LCPC). He earned his Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of Tennessee, Knoxville, and his M.S. in Mathematics and B.S. in Computer Science from the American University of Beirut. His research spans graph algorithms, combinatorics, artificial intelligence, and high-performance computing, including record-breaking kernelization bounds and fast exact and enumeration algorithms for classical graph problems.

Latif Ladid

Founder and President of the IPv6 Forum and a leading advocate for next-generation internet technologies. He is an Emeritus Trustee of the Internet Society (ISOC), leads the IPv6 Ready Logo and IPv6 Education programs, and is a Senior Researcher at the University of Luxembourg (SnT) and a board member in global standardization bodies including 3GPP. He is widely recognized as an international pioneer in IPv6 deployment, IoT, and future networks.

Call for Papers

Submission Guidelines

Submissions are encouraged, but not limited to, topics spanning informatics education, problem solving, and competitive programming coaching.

Authors are invited to submit original, previously unpublished research papers describing particular challenges or experiences, or proposing novel solutions relevant to the scope of the conference. Papers must present original and unpublished work and should not be currently under review by any other conference or journal. Papers should be written in English and formatted according to the Springer LNCS one-column page format, with a length of 12–15 pages. For more detailed guidelines, please visit the official Springer Guidelines website.

ICIPS 2026 accepts full research papers (original and unpublished research contributions), short papers (ongoing work or innovative ideas), and posters / demos (practical implementations, case studies, and prototypes). The proceedings of all accepted and presented papers are planned to be published by Springer in the Lecture Notes in Networks and Systems (LNNS) series (approval waiting), subject to Springer's quality checks, and submitted for consideration in Web of Science and indexes including SCOPUS, SCImago, INSPEC, WTI Frankfurt eG, the Norwegian Register for Scientific Journals and Series, and zbMATH.

All submitted papers undergo double-blind peer review by at least two independent reviewers from the Program Committee, evaluated on originality, relevance, technical quality, clarity of presentation, and contribution to the field. Submissions are also checked for plagiarism, with reports required to stay below 15%.

The Microsoft CMT service was used for managing the peer-reviewing process for this conference. This service was provided for free by Microsoft and they bore all expenses, including costs for Azure cloud services as well as for software development and support.

Registration & Fees

Two registration types are offered: in-person attendance at the venue, or online attendance via a Google Meet link.

In-Person Registration is for attendees who plan to physically attend the conference at the designated venue — networking with fellow researchers, engaging in interactive discussions, and experiencing the atmosphere of the event. Online Registration is for authors unable to attend in person: via a Google Meet link, you can join the conference virtually, participate in sessions, and present your research remotely.
